Module 1

Purpose, audience, and document planning

Technical Writing and Documentation uses purpose, audience, and document planning as a building block for more advanced study. Start by understanding the plain-language purpose of the idea, the vocabulary attached to it, and the kinds of questions it helps us answer. In beginner-friendly terms, this module asks: what problem does this concept solve, and why would a computing professional care about it?

At a deeper level, we connect purpose, audience, and document planning to the broader communication pathway. We examine the assumptions behind the concept, how it interacts with adjacent topics in Semester 3: Algorithms, Databases, and Systems, and what changes when systems become larger, faster, more distributed, or more safety-critical. Strong students should look for trade-offs, limits, and edge cases rather than memorizing a single rule.

In practice, teams apply purpose, audience, and document planning while building or improving a student information platform. The value of the topic becomes obvious when design choices need to be justified, tested, documented, and communicated to collaborators.

Scenario: You are advising a small team that needs to apply technical writing and documentation to a realistic problem with clear constraints and measurable outcomes.

Requirements

  • Use at least three major topics from the course and explain why each one matters.
  • Create a concrete artifact such as a program, design, model, report, experiment, or architecture proposal.
  • Include testing, evaluation, or reflection criteria so quality can be judged transparently.
  • Document assumptions, trade-offs, and future improvements.

Step-by-Step Build Plan

  1. Choose a realistic problem statement and define what success looks like.
  2. Break the work into milestones tied to specific course concepts.
  3. Build and review the core artifact iteratively rather than all at once.
  4. Evaluate the result with evidence such as tests, traces, feedback, or metrics.
  5. Package the final deliverable with documentation and a presentation-ready summary.

Deliverables: A final artifact, short technical report, demonstration summary, and evaluation evidence for Technical Writing and Documentation.

Rubric

  • Problem framing and relevance
  • Correct use of course concepts
  • Technical quality or analytical rigor

  • Reflection, testing, and improvement planning
