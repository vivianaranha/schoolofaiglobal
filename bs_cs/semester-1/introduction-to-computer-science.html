  • Explain the scope and history of computing within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Apply hardware, software, and digital system components within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Analyze data representation, storage, and information processing within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Design operating systems and application software roles within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Evaluate networks, the web, and cloud-based computing within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Interpret programming as a tool for automation and problem solving within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Implement algorithms, abstraction, and computational thinking within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Compare cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and emerging technologies within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Troubleshoot careers, roles, and collaborative work in computing within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.
  • Connect ethics, accessibility, and the social impact of technology within realistic introduction to computer science scenarios.

The scope and history of computing

Introduction to Computer Science uses the scope and history of computing as a building block for more advanced study. Start by understanding the plain-language purpose of the idea, the vocabulary attached to it, and the kinds of questions it helps us answer. In beginner-friendly terms, this module asks: what problem does this concept solve, and why would a computing professional care about it?

At a deeper level, we connect the scope and history of computing to the broader foundations pathway. We examine the assumptions behind the concept, how it interacts with adjacent topics in Semester 1: Foundations of Computing, and what changes when systems become larger, faster, more distributed, or more safety-critical.
