Learning Outcomes

By the end of this subject, a self-paced learner should be able to do the following with confidence.

  • Explain what hci studies and why usability matters within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Apply user-centered design and problem framing within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Analyze user research, personas, and journey mapping within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Design information architecture and navigation clarity within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Evaluate interaction design patterns and feedback loops within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Interpret usability heuristics and cognitive load within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Implement accessibility standards and inclusive design within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Compare wireframes, prototypes, and design iteration within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Troubleshoot usability testing and interpretation of findings within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.
  • Connect ethical design and long-term product trust within realistic human-computer interaction scenarios.

Module 1

What HCI studies and why usability matters

Human-Computer Interaction uses what hci studies and why usability matters as a building block for more advanced study. Start by understanding the plain-language purpose of the idea, the vocabulary attached to it, and the kinds of questions it helps us answer. In beginner-friendly terms, this module asks: what problem does this concept solve, and why would a computing professional care about it?

At a deeper level, we connect what hci studies and why usability matters to the broader leadership and ethics pathway. We examine the assumptions behind the concept, how it interacts with adjacent topics in Semester 4: Software Development and Networking, and what changes when systems become larger, faster, more distributed, or more safety-critical. Strong students should look for trade-offs, limits, and edge cases rather than memorizing a single rule.

In practice, teams apply what hci studies and why usability matters while building or improving a student information platform. The value of the topic becomes obvious when design choices need to be justified, tested, documented, and communicated to collaborators.
