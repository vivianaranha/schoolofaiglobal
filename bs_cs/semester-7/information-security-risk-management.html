Learning Outcomes

By the end of this subject, a self-paced learner should be able to do the following with confidence.

  • Explain information security goals and governance structures within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Apply risk identification, analysis, and prioritization within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Analyze frameworks, controls, and policy design within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Design identity and access management strategy within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Evaluate compliance, audit, and evidence requirements within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Interpret business continuity and resilience planning within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Implement disaster recovery and service restoration within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Compare vendor, supply-chain, and third-party risk within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Troubleshoot awareness training and culture building within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.
  • Connect metrics, reporting, and executive communication within realistic information security and risk management scenarios.

Module 1

Information security goals and governance structures

Information Security and Risk Management uses information security goals and governance structures as a building block for more advanced study. Start by understanding the plain-language purpose of the idea, the vocabulary attached to it, and the kinds of questions it helps us answer. In beginner-friendly terms, this module asks: what problem does this concept solve, and why would a computing professional care about it?

At a deeper level, we connect information security goals and governance structures to the broader security pathway. We examine the assumptions behind the concept, how it interacts with adjacent topics in Semester 7: Specialization and Industry Readiness, and what changes when systems become larger, faster, more distributed, or more safety-critical. Strong students should look for trade-offs, limits, and edge cases rather than memorizing a single rule.

In practice, teams apply information security goals and governance structures while building or improving a student information platform. The value of the topic becomes obvious when design choices need to be justified, tested, documented, and communicated to collaborators.
